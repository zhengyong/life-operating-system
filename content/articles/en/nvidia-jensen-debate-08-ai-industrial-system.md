**The case for succession | National competitiveness · U.S.–China AI competition and industrial capacity**

### Introduction

AI is becoming an important force in global technology competition and industrial change.

Can the future of the U.S. AI industry really depend on whether one company’s CEO stays in office?

**One-sentence summary: U.S. AI competitiveness depends on a system of technology, capital and manufacturing that extends far beyond any one entrepreneur.**

I want to make five points.

### 1. The U.S.–China AI race is a contest between industrial systems

GPUs are one part of AI competition.

The race also involves large models, cloud computing, semiconductor manufacturing, power, networking, software, talent and research institutions.

The United States has many technology companies and research institutions. China continues to build out its AI industry.

**One CEO’s career decision cannot determine the outcome of a contest this large.**

### 2. NVIDIA matters within a much bigger American AI industry

The United States has Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic and many companies in semiconductors, manufacturing and energy.

NVIDIA is a major computing platform provider. In-house chips from cloud companies, AMD and other computing architectures also compete.

**The strength of the AI industry comes from many companies innovating continuously.**

A strong NVIDIA matters, but U.S. AI development cannot assume that any individual CEO is irreplaceable.

### 3. Rebuilding American manufacturing depends on lasting investment and supply chains

Semiconductor plants, advanced packaging, AI servers, power infrastructure and data centers all require long-term capital and partnerships across companies.

These projects often take years and involve engineering teams, suppliers and contractual arrangements across many businesses.

**A CEO change does not erase the factories that have already been built or put every infrastructure plan back at the starting line.**

Manufacturing competitiveness ultimately rests on sustained investment, engineering capability and efficient production.

### 4. Sound corporate governance is part of national competitiveness

A country’s high-tech industries need stable governance, compliance and risk management alongside continuous innovation.

Depending too much on one leader creates key-person risk.

**Over the long run, turning a founder’s talents into ways of working that endure may matter more than keeping the founder in charge indefinitely.**

A smooth transition at NVIDIA would demonstrate the resilience of a major U.S. technology company beyond any one individual.

### 5. National competitiveness depends on the ability to keep innovating

From 2025 through 2030, AI training, inference and agents are likely to keep driving demand for computing power.

After 2030, robotics, autonomous driving and industrial AI may open up further opportunities.

Those possibilities are unlikely to disappear when one entrepreneur leaves.

NVIDIA still needs a good leader. It also has technical teams, a customer ecosystem, research capabilities and a long-term strategic plan.

**America’s AI industry needs a system that can keep producing strong companies and leaders.**

### Summary

History should give Huang full credit for what he did for NVIDIA and AI.

**One entrepreneur’s greatness does not make the development of an entire national industry depend on him.**

A genuinely strong U.S. AI industry should be able to absorb ordinary leadership transitions at its major companies and keep innovating.

**My judgment is that Huang can leave NVIDIA, the company can continue developing and the U.S. AI industry can still move in its established direction.**

**For readers to consider. Is a country stronger for having an irreplaceable entrepreneur, or for having institutions that keep producing exceptional entrepreneurs?**
