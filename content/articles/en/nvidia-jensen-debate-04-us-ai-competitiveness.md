**The case for Huang | National competitiveness · AI competition and rebuilding U.S. manufacturing**

### Introduction

If AI determines future industrial competitiveness, NVIDIA matters beyond the stock market. Huang’s departure deserves to be considered in the wider context of America’s AI industry.

**One-sentence summary: America’s AI industry needs leading computing platforms and leaders who can bring technology, capital and manufacturing together.**

Why take Huang’s departure so seriously? I have five reasons.

### 1. The U.S.–China AI race goes beyond large models

The United States is promoting AI infrastructure, innovation and advanced manufacturing. China is advancing its AI Plus initiative and building out intelligent computing capacity.

The competition is spreading from model capabilities to data centers, robotics, advanced manufacturing, scientific research and other industries.

**A country’s future competitiveness depends on whether it can turn AI into productivity at scale and keep doing so over time.**

This industrial competition could last more than a decade.

### 2. NVIDIA is a foundation of the U.S. AI industry

The United States has strong technology companies including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Amazon.

They bring different strengths to models, cloud computing and applications. NVIDIA supplies GPUs, networking, systems and software that much AI infrastructure relies on.

**NVIDIA’s technology choices and its ability to deliver are closely tied to the pace of the U.S. AI industry.**

A major innovation or execution failure at NVIDIA could ripple through the whole supply chain.

### 3. Rebuilding U.S. manufacturing takes more than investment

The United States wants to expand advanced semiconductor manufacturing, server production and data center infrastructure.

From NVIDIA and TSMC’s work on U.S. Blackwell production to AI servers, networking and energy infrastructure, investment in computing is reaching more parts of manufacturing.

Building factories is the first step.

**The deeper challenge is bringing chip design, advanced manufacturing, supply chains and global demand into a system that works efficiently.**

Huang has long played a strategic coordination role in that system.

### 4. America has technology and capital. Great industrial leaders are scarce too

Scientists drive discoveries, engineers bring products to market and capital builds infrastructure.

Someone still has to pick a technology direction for the next decade, coordinate investments that run into hundreds of billions or trillions of dollars, and take a large company through successive technology cycles.

**Governments can set industrial strategy. Companies have to turn it into products, factories and business results.**

Huang is one of the leading business figures in the U.S. AI industry.

### 5. Why take on succession risk at a pivotal moment for AI?

From generative AI to agents, self-driving vehicles, robotics and physical AI, the industry is still changing quickly.

NVIDIA must keep making consequential choices about products, research, supply chains and investment.

A change of CEO may take time to show up in the financial statements.

**The risk I worry about is a loss of strategic continuity that slows execution through the next few technology cycles.**

For a company, this is a change of CEO. For the industry, it is a leadership transition at a critical infrastructure supplier.

### Summary

The United States wants AI to strengthen its technological competitiveness, support advanced manufacturing and help it lead in the industries of the future.

NVIDIA is an important participant. Huang has been central to its strategic development for more than three decades.

**AI competitiveness comes from technology and capital and from leaders who can keep innovation, manufacturing and commercial partnerships moving together.**

The U.S. AI industry will not immediately lose its edge when Huang leaves.

At this moment, how good must the reasons be to justify taking on the risks of a leadership change at NVIDIA?

**My view is that NVIDIA’s future has consequences far beyond NVIDIA itself.**

**For readers to consider. When a company supplies essential infrastructure to a national industry, should its succession be considered an internal decision alone, or weighed for its wider effects?**
