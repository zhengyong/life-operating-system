**My judgment | From company value to the future of the AI industry**

### Introduction

Eight essays, two positions and four rounds of debate.

**Can NVIDIA stay ahead? Who can take over from Huang? Will its global partnerships change? What happens to America’s AI competitiveness?**

The case for Huang is that he is extremely hard to replace. The case for succession is that a mature company should be able to outlast any individual leader.

Both arguments have a logic of their own. I think the case for succession overlooks the most important question. **A company can keep operating and still pay a heavy price.**

**One-sentence summary: NVIDIA will eventually have to replace Jensen Huang. Losing him in this crucial decade for AI could come at a significant cost to NVIDIA, the global AI industry and U.S. advanced manufacturing.**

Here are my five judgments.

---

### 1. The technical debate. CUDA will remain. Who will create the next CUDA?

The case for succession is that NVIDIA will keep its GPUs, CUDA, research teams and roadmap after Huang leaves.

**That is true. These are already achievements NVIDIA can draw on.**

NVIDIA’s advantage depends on the choices it has made so far and on its ability to keep identifying the next promising direction.

From GPUs to CUDA and from AI training to full systems, Huang has been valuable because he has kept helping NVIDIA redefine itself.

**Inheriting yesterday’s NVIDIA and creating tomorrow’s NVIDIA require very different abilities.**

### 2. The leadership debate. NVIDIA has strong executives. Can they take over Huang’s full role?

The case for succession is that NVIDIA’s mature team means the company can proceed without finding another Huang.

I think that is exactly where the difficulty lies.

**Excellent engineers, business leaders and professional managers may be plentiful. A founder with technical vision, organizational leadership, commercial judgment and influence across the industry is rare.**

Even if a prominent technology leader such as Musk or Altman were to take over, there is no guarantee that they could do the job well right away.

The unusually flat system Huang shaped over more than three decades relies heavily on how well he coordinates across these areas.

**Appointing another CEO is much easier than reproducing what Huang brings to the job.**

### 3. The partnership debate. Contracts will survive. What is the value of industry trust?

The case for succession is that TSMC, Microsoft, Amazon and OpenAI work with NVIDIA because of commercial interests that go beyond personal relationships.

I agree.

**Commercial partners also have to judge the future together, take risks and coordinate resources. Contracts are one part of that work.**

How will capacity for advanced chips be allocated? Who will plan the next computing architecture? How will companies building models and cloud providers coordinate their investment over the next few years?

A single contract cannot answer all of those questions.

In more than three decades, Huang has accumulated more than contacts. The global AI industry has come to trust the way he makes long-term strategic decisions and delivers on them.

**Customers will not leave NVIDIA immediately. Even if they stay, losing a leader who can move an entire industry forward together comes at a cost.**

### 4. The national debate. America can carry on without one person. Can it afford the opportunity cost?

The case for succession is that a country with Google, Microsoft, Amazon, OpenAI and many other businesses cannot depend on Huang alone for its AI competitiveness.

That is also true.

Still, the competitiveness of a national industry is built from individual companies, technologies and people that matter.

**NVIDIA is one of the most important computing infrastructure suppliers in the U.S. AI industry.**

The United States is building AI infrastructure and advanced manufacturing capacity while China accelerates its own AI development. NVIDIA’s speed of execution matters far beyond the company itself.

**America will not lose the AI race just because it loses Huang. It could still give up part of its competitive edge over the next decade.**

Is that cost not worth considering carefully?

### 5. The cost is already coming into view

Some argue that NVIDIA can keep operating normally after Huang leaves.

The company reported about $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue on November 17 and gave revenue guidance of roughly $130 billion for the following quarter. The core business remains strong.

**The results largely reflect operations before Huang’s departure. They cannot establish that the transition carries no cost.**

Financial markets have already reacted.

On November 12, NVIDIA shares closed down 4.3% after a steeper intraday decline. Some other AI-related shares also fell.

A decline in the stock price does not permanently erase an equal amount of company assets. It is also impossible to attribute every move to the change of CEO.

It does suggest that **the market has begun to reassess NVIDIA’s exposure to key-person risk.**

The largest costs may take years to come into view.

A wrong turn in technology, a delay in R&D spending or a missed chance at a major industry partnership could cost much more than any short-term stock move.

**The absence of lost orders today tells us little about the opportunity cost over the coming years.**

---

### My judgment. NVIDIA will have a second CEO. Now is the wrong time

First, Huang is subject to the law, corporate governance and board oversight. Any question involving compliance, conflicts of interest or national security should be investigated lawfully, evaluated independently and followed by appropriate accountability.

**Holding someone accountable and preserving a company’s ability to compete are both questions that deserve serious answers.**

After the eight essays, I am more certain of four points.

**First, Huang can eventually be replaced.** NVIDIA will ultimately need to hand over leadership.

**Second, continuity today tells us little about long-term strategic costs.** A technical lead today cannot guarantee an edge ten years from now.

**Third, 2025 to 2035 is a crucial window for AI.** Training, inference, agents, robots, autonomous driving and physical AI are reshaping computing together.

**Fourth, Huang’s importance in this decade cannot be captured by a CEO title, his pay or a set of financial statements.**

I expect NVIDIA to have a successor.

**My objection is to discounting the cost of losing Huang before the new leadership has shown it can make strategic decisions as effectively.**

NVIDIA may remain a great company.

America may still have a strong AI industry.

If Huang led NVIDIA for another decade, how much further might the company and the industry be able to go?

**Some of the deepest losses are futures that could have happened and never came to pass.**

### Summary

After weighing the case for Huang and the case for succession in the eight essays, I come back to one judgment.

**Losing Jensen Huang is a loss for NVIDIA. It could also be a loss for the global AI industry and American advanced manufacturing.**

We cannot quantify the full cost over the next ten years today.

Many of the effects may not have occurred yet and may not show up in the current financial statements. That is no reason to dismiss them.

**NVIDIA will eventually have a second CEO.**

**Given what the next decade of AI has at stake, I think this is the wrong time to hand over leadership.**

That is my judgment.

### For readers to consider

**If NVIDIA is still growing rapidly five years from now, would that prove Huang was less important than it seemed?**

**If NVIDIA is still a strong company ten years from now, how could we tell whether it missed a future in which it could have been greater still?**
